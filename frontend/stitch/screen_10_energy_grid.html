Autonomous Dynamic Switching for High-Load Campus Feeders

During peak research hours (10:30 AM to 3:30 PM), heavy inductive loads from the Mechanical Engineering workshops, CAD labs, and Materials Testing rigs create erratic power swings. The NIE campus currently incurs punitive maximum demand charges from CHESCOM (Chamundeshwari Electricity Supply Corporation) and falls back on high-emission 250 kVA diesel generator sets during unscheduled feeder switchovers.

This capstone initiative pioneers an edge-directed microgrid coordinator. By sampling power transducers in Substation 1 & 2 every 100 milliseconds, our edge algorithms dynamically discharge a 24 kWh LiFePO4 battery buffer to shave peak surges, while automatically decoupling low-priority air conditioning circuits via solid-state relays without interrupting sensitive server and lab loads.

Microgrid Telemetry & Actuation Topology

01

Distributed High-Frequency Power Transducers (Substation 1 & 2)

ESP32-S3 custom carrier boards connected via Modbus RTU (RS485) to Schneider PM5350 power meters. Samples true-RMS active/reactive power, power factor, and harmonics at 10 Hz across bus couplers.

02

Intranet InfluxDB & Mosquitto MQTT Broker (CentOS 8 Server)

Lightweight edge telemetry ingest handling 1,200 data points/sec with automatic local ring-buffering in SPIFFS memory to ensure zero telemetry dropout during campus Wi-Fi handshakes.

03

Predictive Battery Scheduling via Edge LSTM Load Forecaster

Python/FastAPI inference pipeline forecasting 30-minute campus demand windows using solar irradiance sensors and timetabled laboratory utilization profiles to determine charge/discharge thresholds.

04

Actuator Relay Bank with Zero-Crossing Solid-State Switches

Optoisolated thyristor-based contactors executing phase-aligned shedding commands under 8 milliseconds, eliminating voltage transients on the sensitive campus mainframe cluster.
